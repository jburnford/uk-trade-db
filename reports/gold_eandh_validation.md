**Hold-out rule.** The gold is a test set. No pipeline value has been changed
because the gold says so, and `reference/gold_eandh.csv` is not cited as
authority in `group_repairs.csv` or `manual_rows.csv`. Fixes prompted by rung 4
must be justified by the page image, our own printed subtotal, or cross-engine
agreement — and say so.

`validate_gold.py` come from the same underlying database, so **1871, 1876,
1881, 1886, 1891, 1896** have been benchmarked before. Every rate below is
given twice; the *naive* row is the honest number.

quinquennial years, are leftovers from the wider five-yearly pull, and are
never summed.

## Rung 4 — adjudication

Four arbiters, none of which is either transcription's assertion about the
disputed cell: the gold's own country sum against its own World row; the same
substitution against **our** Tier-1 anchor (printed on a different page, read by
two engines); whether the *other* column agrees; and the digit shape.

| verdict | n |
|---|---|
| pipeline_error | 18 |
| gold_year_unreliable | 5 |
| gold_error | 3 |

### 1898 — settled without the page image

Gold Argentine Republic reads q 64,087; we read 164,037. The **values agree to
50**, so both sides read the same printed row. Substituting our quantity into
the gold's own country rows gives

```
1,921,991 − 64,087 + 164,037 = 2,021,941
```

which is our Tier-1 anchor for 1898 **exactly**. So the gold slipped twice on
one page — a lost leading digit in Argentina, and 2,621,941 for 2,021,941 in
its World row — and our OCR is right both times.

### 1897 — a digit that moved between adjacent rows, and it is ours

The gold's country rows sum to 1,950,925, exactly the Tier-1 anchor that our
own OCR independently reads. Ours sum to 1,953,168. Two cells carry it:

| cell | gold | ours | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Argentine Republic | 167,625 | 107,025 | −60,600 |
| United States (Atlantic) | 200,602 | 260,602 | +60,000 |

A `6` absent from our Argentina and present in our United States. Our block
sums to within 0.11% of the anchor, so the 0.1% closure test barely fails it and
the 5% test passes — this is precisely the defect class internal arithmetic
cannot see. Three value cells in the same 1897 block are also truncated (China
11,825→1,195; Chile 1,120→112; Other Foreign 3,790→357).

### A label defect the gold identified

Our 1884 tallow table carries a country called **`Wales`** at 204,282 cwt. The
gold's `Australasia New South Wales` reads **204,282** cwt — exact. `Wales` is a
truncated `New South Wales`; the crosswalk now records it with that evidence.
The value differs by one digit (gold 380,542, ours 360,542) and the gold's own
1884 arithmetic favours the gold's reading.

## Cross-reference: the single-digit repair method

`scripts/digit_repair_candidates.py` proposes corrections from printed
subtotals alone — `delta = printed TOTAL − sum(members)`, kept when exactly one
member differs from `member + delta` in one digit position. Of the 15 tallow
candidates, the gold speaks to 5 and is silent on 4 (deduplicating engine
variants):

| year | cell | col | we read | proposed | gold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1891 | Holland | v | 30,943 | **30,043** | 30,043 ✓ |
| 1889 | Australasia | q | 481,169 | **431,169** | 431,169 ✓ |
| 1893 | New Zealand | v | 216,239 | **246,239** | 246,239 ✓ |
| 1874 | Other Countries | q | 92,095 | **22,095** | 22,095 ✓ |
| 1892 | United States | v | 382,486 | **582,486** | 582,486 ✓ |

**Five confirmed, none refuted.** An arithmetic inference from a printed
subtotal, corroborated by an independent human transcription, in every case
where the gold has an opinion. Our shipped 1891 Holland cell already carries
30,043.

## What this run did not do

- **No page images were consulted.** 1875, 1897, 1898 and 1884 were settled by
  arithmetic against a total printed on a different page, which is stronger
  than it sounds but is not the plate. The 5 `gold_year_unreliable` cells and
  the 1883/1891 Australasia residuals are still open and would need the image.
- **The 18 `pipeline_error` cells have not been fixed** — the hold-out rule.
  They are a worklist, in `reports/gold_eandh_adjudication.csv`.
- Rung 1 covers 1868–1870 only through the `tn_1871` comparative columns; those
  three years sit within 5% but not within 0.1% and are not corpus-proper.

## Reproducibility

`scripts/ingest_gold_eandh.py` reproduces `reference/gold_eandh.csv`
byte-identically and records both hashes. The workbook's own wide summary
sheets (in tons) agree with the raw sheets on every overlapping year, so the
BIFF read is corroborated internally.

It needs `xlrd` (`python3 -m pip install --break-system-packages xlrd`); the
script exits with that instruction if the import is missing. `map_slim.json`
was found to be **non-deterministic across runs** — three runs gave three byte
hashes with identical content, from set-iteration order — so this validation
reads it for content only and never for a hash.
