How to read this / what is not certain

  • 1871 is a genuine hole, not a dip — no volume covering that year exists in the corpus. The line is broken rather than interpolated.
  • 1897–1900 is weakly corroborated (4–17%). Those four years are the only ones with no single-year volume, and their figures come from wide comparative tables whose right-hand column loses digits at the page edge. The values are accurate to roughly 2.7% at the median — good enough for the trend, not for precise levels.
  • 1874 was repaired. One OCR cell fused two printed columns and read £45.27 bn; corrected from the raw text to £94,110, putting 1874 at £8.83M — where its neighbours predict.
  • Article coverage varies by year (93–206 commodities; shown in the tooltip). Part of a fall can be a fall in what the parser recovered, so read the count alongside the value.
  • The United States and British East Indies are excluded from the comparison: they still carry uncorrected fused-digit cells (US 1872/73/76/80, BEI 1874). The four shown are clean.
  • “Corroborated” means the cell sits inside a printed section whose members sum exactly to the subtotal the compositor printed beside them. There is no external gold set for exports; the page proves itself.
