# The mirror: UK exports to Canada vs Canadian imports from Great Britain

Generated by `scripts/mirror_canada_uk.py`. A ratio-band instrument, never a closure proof: the UK side is a calendar year, the Canadian side is blended from two fiscal years (1 July - 30 June) per `COMPLETION_PLAN.md` section 1. Sterling converted at GBP1 = $4.8666. UK side = British produce + foreign re-exports, own-year rows of the parsed destination tables (no printed by-destination total exists in the corpus, so the UK side is a floor). Canadian side = value imported from Great Britain, parsed General Statement; the "printed" column uses the prefatory by-country series of value entered for consumption (the Canadian side at print, 1873-89).

**Years with both sides: 19. Pass: 15. Flag: 4.** Pass = blended single-year ratio within +-15% and three-year ratio within +-8% (or only one test available); "pass (one test)" = one of the two.

Geography caveat: through 1896 the UK label is "British North America", which includes Newfoundland; the Canadian side never does. The UK side therefore carries an upward bias before 1897 (stated, not corrected). From 1897 the Newfoundland rows are excluded (column `Nfld GBP`).

## Reading the flags

A ratio above 1 means Canada recorded more arriving from Britain than Britain recorded sending. Small excesses are expected (freight and insurance are not in either value, but Canadian "fair market value" runs a little above f.o.b.; the UK side is a floor). A ratio far above 1 with the Canadian side at print (printed ratio agrees) points at the UK export parse for that year; a ratio far below 1 points at the Canadian parse. Confirm any single-year flag with the three-year column before acting on it.

Flagged years: 1870 (0.56, 3-yr -), 1872 (0.80, 3-yr -), 1873 (1.19, 3-yr 1.11), 1874 (1.39, 3-yr 1.19).
