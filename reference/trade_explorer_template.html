UK Trade Coverage Explorer, 1866–1900

Every import cell in the database, from double-keyed OCR of the Annual Statements of Trade. Each distinct commodity is its own series — labels merge only when they are the same words reordered or OCR-mangled (“Sheep and Lambs’ Wool” ≡ “Wool — Sheep and Lambs’”), never across real category lines (goats’ wool stays separate from sheep’s). Each commodity opens on its national total (Tier-1 voted, 1866–1900); pick an origin for by-country detail (1872–1899). The source also changed its category boundaries over time (“Butter and Butterine” 1878–84, split thereafter) — such related series are offered as labelled chips you can append deliberately, never merged for you.

Inspect a commodity

Rank 2 means the cell is corroborated without a second printing: its country block sums to the printed annual total on the page, both OCR engines read the same digits, or its unit price sits in the series band. Rank 3 has no proof. A missing year-cell means the parse lost it (e.g. Gutta Percha 1873: the country rows were read but the quantity column came back empty) or the source didn’t enumerate the commodity separately that year. Reproduce any view in SQL against country_year_final: WHERE q_rank <= 2. Never compare quantities across different units.
