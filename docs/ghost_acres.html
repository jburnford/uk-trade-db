Canada's Ghost Acres

Where in the world was the land behind what Canada consumed by way of Britain — the tea and silk and wine that Britain merely forwarded, and the cotton fields, sheep runs, cane and beet, flax, ore and hides inside the goods Britain made?

Declared values at British ports, current £, summed 1870–1900. Value stands in for acreage — see the caveats — and every foreign figure is an allocation under stated assumptions, not a reading.

The big picture

Places

The twenty largest origins, with what carried the value there. Australasia folds the seven colonies; British East Indies folds Bombay, Madras, Bengal, Burmah, Ceylon and the Straits; the United States folds its Atlantic and Pacific coasts.

Materials

The raw materials inside British-made goods sent to Canada, with the UK's whole-period import origins for each. These origin mixes are what the map redistributes.

Assumptions, commodity by commodity

Every British-produce commodity was assigned a raw material and a split between imported and home-grown material; the imported part follows the UK's import origins for that material over the whole period. The splits are round period-typical figures and are the part of this exercise most worth arguing with.

What this is and is not

Value, not acres — deliberately. The measure is declared value at British ports, and it stays that way. Land intensity differs enormously by material — a pound's worth of wool, cotton, sugar, tea or flax stands for far more land than a pound's worth of Spanish ore or Chilean copper — but converting value to acreage means multiplying by yield coefficients that are themselves the weakest link in ghost-acre accounting. This page is a foundation layer: it locates the geography and puts the traceable value on it; the more careful analysis and the narrative are built on top of it commodity by commodity, not by a coefficient.

Allocation, not observation. The Statement records destination for exports and origin for imports and never both together. Both layers spread Canada's value over the UK's import mix as a whole; the material splits for British goods are assumptions.

Consignment, not production. The import tables name the port goods were shipped from. Holland and Belgium stand in for the Rhine hinterland and beyond; Hong Kong for southern China; Gibraltar for the Mediterranean.

What the lens cannot see. Anything Canada bought directly — American goods across the border, Indian and Ceylon tea consigned to Montreal, the Pacific route after 1886 — is invisible here. This is the geography of the British-mediated part of Canadian consumption only.

Data state. Own-year sections close on their printed totals in 63.8% of cases (80% within 5%); 1871 is missing and 1897–1900 are the weakest years. Zinc and iron on the re-export side include a known misfiled-table class.
